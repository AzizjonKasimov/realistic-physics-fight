Fight

Two boxers seen from the side, on a realistic injury simulation: step in, jab, cross, punch to the body, block, duck, and lean back against a computer opponent, over three one-minute rounds. Every blow hurts skin, muscle and bone through real physics, and a hard one to the jaw can knock a fighter out.

Content warning: graphic simulated injury, including blood, torn tissue and broken bones.

About

A fight is three rounds of a minute. A fighter knocked down gets up on its own while the referee counts: up by ten, the fight goes on after the count of eight; still down at ten is a knockout, and three knockdowns end the fight. Otherwise the fighter who landed more clean punches wins: on the head or the body rather than the arms, and landing hard. Punching tires your arms: the bar under your name shows what they have left, tired arms punch too slow to count, and resting brings the strength back. Punching opens your guard until the punch is back. The opponent sees what you do a moment late, as a person would, and makes mistakes. R starts the next fight. Fights decided by their other injuries come next.
