# Changelog

Each version of the Fight page, newest first. The page shows the newest
entry's lines as written.

## v0.6 (2026-11-17)

- Fight with a game controller: a PS5 DualSense, a PS4 DualShock 4, an Xbox controller, or any other your browser reads. Press any of its buttons to connect it.
- The D-pad or left stick steps (hold it to keep stepping), leans back, and ducks; L2 (LT) jabs and R2 (RT) crosses, as square and triangle (X and Y) do; R1 or L1 (RB or LB) blocks; OPTIONS (MENU) pauses.
- The first controller plays your fighter, a second the right fighter; CREATE (VIEW) switches a controller to the other fighter, so a keyboard and a controller can fight each other either way round.
- The buttons show your controller's own symbols, and the keys again when you press a key.
- Your controller rumbles when your punch lands, harder when you are hit, and most when you go down. Chrome and Edge cannot rumble a DualSense on Windows by themselves: the first time you press one of its buttons, your browser opens a window to pick it; pick it once, and the page drives it directly. The start card and a button during the fight offer it too.

## v0.5 (2026-11-17)

- A fighter knocked down gets up on its own: from its back it sits up, crouches, and stands; from its front it comes up onto its knees, takes a knee, and stands.
- A knockdown starts the referee's count while the other fighter waits at its edge of the ring. Up by ten, the fight goes on once the count reaches eight; still down at ten is a knockout.
- The harder the punch, the longer a fighter stays down: most knockdowns are beaten, the hardest punches still end the fight.
- Three knockdowns end the fight. A fall with no punch before it is a slip and is not counted.

## v0.4 (2026-11-17)

- A jab or cross thrown while you hold DUCK goes to the body, under the other fighter's block.
- Hold BLOCK while ducking for a low block: your forearms cover your body, and your head is open.
- The computer opponent goes to the body about one punch in four, more when your guard is up, and blocks low against punches to the body.
- A punch to the body counts as a clean punch.

## v0.3 (2026-11-16)

- A computer opponent fights the right fighter. It sees what you do a moment late, as a person would: it covers up or leans back as you step in, counters when you punch, follows you when you back off, and makes mistakes.
- Pick Easy, Normal, or Hard on the start card, or with the level button at the top.
- A fight is three rounds of a minute. A knockout or a fall ends it at once; otherwise the fighter who landed more clean punches wins.
- Punching opens your guard until the punch is back, for both fighters.

## v0.2 (2026-11-16)

- The fighters now stand side-on, facing each other, on their own legs, and move like boxers: they step in and back, jab, cross, duck, and lean back.
- A punch goes between the other fighter's open hands and lands on its head. Hold BLOCK and your forearms stop it.
- Punches are as fast and as heavy as a trained boxer's.
- A punch that turns the head hard enough knocks a fighter out. It goes down and stays down, and the fight is over: press R to fight again.
- The sparring partner comes into reach on its own, punches, and sometimes covers up, ducks, or steps back.
- New keys: W A S D to move, J K L to jab, cross, and block; a friend takes the right fighter with the arrows and the comma, full stop, and slash keys.
- On a phone your seven moves are buttons; a friend now plays the right fighter from the keyboard only.

## v0.1.2 (2026-10-20)

- Punching again and again, or punching from a lean, no longer tears your fighter's wrist, elbow, or lower back.
- Punches on the other fighter's raised guard no longer tear its elbow or shoulder, and tear its wrist far less often.

## v0.1.1 (2026-10-19)

- Punches thrown at nearly the same moment no longer crush the fighters' arms: the guards bump and both punches come back.
- The fighters' feet stay on the floor when they punch or lean back, instead of a leg swinging out.

## v0.1 (2026-10-19)

- Fight: you play the left fighter. Punch with D or the right arrow, and hold A or the left arrow to lean back so a punch falls short.
- The right fighter spars on its own, throwing a punch every few seconds, until a friend takes it over with J and L.
- On a phone, tap PUNCH and hold LEAN BACK; a friend can take the buttons on the right.
- Nobody wins yet: blows hurt the fighters as they hurt bodies in the sandbox, and R starts the fight afresh.
